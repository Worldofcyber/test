About

Security advice for the other 99% of us.

Most security content is written for security professionals, by security professionals. That's fine for security professionals. It's not much use to the rest of us — the parent trying to figure out if a text about a late package is real, the small-business owner who is also the IT department, the person staring at a breach notification with no idea what to do in the next hour.

CyberWorld exists to close that gap. Every briefing is written to answer one question: what should a person with no security background actually do, right now, about this specific threat?

How we decide what to cover

We prioritize threats by how many ordinary people they're likely to reach and how much damage they do when they land — not by how technically interesting they are. A widespread SMS scam gets covered before an exotic vulnerability that only affects enterprise servers.

How we rate severity

Every briefing carries a threat-level tag — Low, Guarded, Elevated, or Critical — reflecting how active and how damaging a threat currently is, not how alarming it sounds. We'd rather under-color a headline than send anyone into a panic they don't need.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell fear. No countdown timers, no "hackers are already inside your phone."
  • We don't recommend products we haven't actually looked at.
  • We don't assume you have a budget, an IT team, or hours to spare.

If a briefing ever leaves you less clear than before you read it, that's a bug. Tell us.
